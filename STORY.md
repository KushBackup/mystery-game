# 🔍 THE ROHAN SHARMA MURDER MYSTERY
## Complete Story Bible & Game Narrative

---

## 📖 PROLOGUE: THE PARTY INVITATION

### The Host: Rohan Sharma

Rohan Sharma, 34, was many things to many people—but rarely anything good. A successful tech entrepreneur who made his fortune through a controversial social media analytics company, Rohan had a reputation that preceded him like a storm cloud. He was the kid who made fun of the stutterer in elementary school, the teenager who spread vicious rumors, the college senior who hazed freshmen mercilessly, and the boss who took credit for his employees' work while publicly humiliating them.

But three months ago, something changed.

Rohan was diagnosed with Stage 4 pancreatic cancer. The doctors gave him six months, maybe less. Suddenly, every cruel word, every betrayal, every person he'd stepped on to climb higher—they all came flooding back. The weight of a lifetime of cruelty pressed down on him like a physical force.

He decided to do something unprecedented: apologize. Not privately, not through letters, but publicly, dramatically, to everyone he'd ever wronged—all at once.

### The Venue: For the Record

"For the Record" is an upscale bar in Panjim, Goa—all exposed brick, vintage records on walls, leather booths, and a long mahogany bar. Rohan rented it out entirely for the evening of January 25th, 2026. He called it his "Redemption Party."

### The Guest List: 32 Victims

Every person invited had one thing in common: Rohan had wronged them. Some he'd bullied in school. Others he'd sabotaged professionally. A few he'd betrayed romantically. One or two he'd financially destroyed. They came from all walks of life—doctors, artists, lawyers, students, entrepreneurs. Most came out of morbid curiosity. Some came for closure. A few came for something darker.

## 🎭 ACT ONE: THE GATHERING (7:00 PM - 8:00 PM)

### The Setup

Esha, Rohan's wife of five years, arrived at 5:00 PM to oversee preparations. She brought several items from home:
- A sealed bottle of 50-year-old Macallan scotch (Rohan's prized possession)
- A special cooler bag with "artisanal ice cubes" she insisted were from a premium ice company
- An elaborate three-tier cake from a local bakery
- Their cat, Whiskers, in a carrier (Rohan claimed the cat "calmed his nerves")

The bar owner, Marcus, thought it was odd that someone would bring their own ice to a bar, but Esha explained these were "spherical whiskey stones" that melted slower and didn't dilute the expensive scotch. She personally placed them in the bar's freezer, in a clearly labeled container: "ROHAN'S ICE - DO NOT USE FOR OTHER DRINKS."

### Guest Arrivals (7:00 PM - 7:45 PM)

The guests trickled in, a parade of people who looked like they'd rather be anywhere else. Some recognized each other from school, others from work, a few from social media rants about "that Rohan guy."

The atmosphere was tense—imagine a room full of people who all hate the same person, waiting for that person to explain why they shouldn't.

---

## 🍷 ACT TWO: THE APOLOGY (8:00 PM - 8:20 PM)

### The Speech

At 8:00 PM sharp, Rohan clinked his glass and asked for everyone's attention. He stood at the head of the room, looking thinner than most remembered, with dark circles under his eyes that makeup couldn't quite hide.

"I know why you're all here," he began. "And I know what you're thinking. 'What angle is Rohan playing now?' 'What does he want from us?' The answer is: nothing. I want nothing from any of you except twenty minutes of your time."

He went through them, one by one—not all 32, but hitting the major groups. The schoolmates he'd tormented. The colleagues he'd sabotaged. The partners he'd cheated. The friends he'd betrayed. With each confession, the room grew quieter.

"I'm not asking for forgiveness," he concluded. "I'm not even asking you to believe I've changed. I'm just asking you to know that I know what I did. And I'm sorry."

### The Toast

At 8:15 PM, Rohan walked behind the bar.

"I've been saving this bottle for twenty years," he said, holding up the Macallan. "I always said I'd open it when I achieved something truly meaningful. Tonight feels right."

He broke the seal—everyone watched—and began pouring glasses. Esha helped distribute them. When all 32 guests had a glass, Rohan made one final pour: his own.

He walked to the freezer, retrieved his container of "special ice," and dropped two spherical cubes into his glass.

"One more thing," he said. "Esha, my love. You've put up with more than anyone should have to. You deserve to taste this first."

He handed her his glass. She took a delicate sip—maybe a teaspoon of liquid—smiled, and handed it back.

"It's perfect," she said.

Rohan raised his glass. "To second chances—or at least, to trying."

Everyone drank.

## 💀 ACT THREE: THE COLLAPSE (8:45 PM)

### The Final Minutes

After the toast, the atmosphere shifted. People began mingling, some even approaching Rohan to talk. The expensive scotch flowed. The cake was cut. For a brief, strange moment, it almost felt like a real party.

Rohan circulated the room, having brief conversations with various guests. He seemed tired but happy. At 8:40 PM, he sat down in a booth, looking pale.

At 8:45 PM, he collapsed.

### The Chaos

The room erupted. Someone shouted for a doctor—Rashmi Shirwant, an ophthalmologist, rushed over but was joined by Dr. Nikita Vaidya, a physiotherapist, and Dr. Akash Halankar, a maritime physician. They tried CPR. Someone called an ambulance.

But it was too late. Rohan Sharma was pronounced dead at 9:02 PM.

### The Police

By 9:30 PM, the bar was locked down. Inspector Maria Fernandes of the Goa Police took charge. A preliminary examination suggested poisoning—Rohan's lips had a slight blue tinge, and his pupils were fixed and dilated.

"Nobody leaves," she announced. "You're all suspects."

## 🔬 THE TRUTH: WHAT REALLY HAPPENED

### The Diagnosis

Three months before the party, Rohan received his death sentence. Stage 4 pancreatic cancer, already metastasized. He had months to live, possibly weeks. The treatments would only prolong his suffering, not save him.

He told only one person: Esha.

### The Journal

Rohan began writing in a journal, processing his mortality. He wrote about his regrets, his cruelties, his wasted life. And then, he wrote something darker:

*"I've decided not to wait for the cancer to take me. I want to choose my moment, on my terms. The party will be my farewell, my apology, and my exit. The scotch will be my final drink. I've already obtained what I need—sodium azide, fast-acting, relatively painless. Esha doesn't know. She can't know. I won't burden her with this."*

But Esha found the journal.

### The Conspiracy

Esha was horrified—but also practical. She loved Rohan, despite everything, and she understood his choice. But she also knew one thing: their life insurance policy, worth ₹5 crores, had a suicide exclusion clause. If Rohan killed himself, she'd get nothing. After his medical bills and debts, she'd be ruined.

She proposed an alternative.

"If you're going to die anyway," she said, "why not make it mean something? Let me help you. We'll make it look like murder. The insurance will pay out. I'll be taken care of. And you'll get your dramatic exit."

Rohan agreed. They planned it together.

### The Method: Poisoned Ice

The plan was elegant:

1. **The Poison**: Sodium azide, obtained from a laboratory supply company using Rohan's tech industry connections. Lethal in small doses, and crucially, takes 20-45 minutes to kill—long enough to establish that others had access to the drink.

2. **The Ice**: Esha froze the poison into spherical ice cubes at home. The poison was distributed throughout the ice, so it would only release as the cubes melted.

3. **The Sip**: Esha would take a tiny sip from Rohan's drink immediately after he added the ice—before significant melting occurred. This would "prove" the drink was safe when served, and she'd only ingest a trace amount (survivable, though she might feel slightly ill later).

4. **The Delay**: The ice would melt over the next 20-30 minutes as Rohan sipped. By the time he'd consumed enough poison, plenty of other people would have had opportunity to "tamper" with his drink.

5. **The Suspects**: The guest list was perfect—32 people who all hated Rohan, any of whom could have slipped something into his drink during the mingling period.

### Why It Works

- The scotch bottle was sealed and opened in front of everyone—clearly not poisoned
- Esha sipped from the glass publicly—clearly safe at that moment
- The glass sat unattended at various points while Rohan circulated
- Every guest had motive to want Rohan dead
- The ice cubes, melted and diluted in the remaining scotch, would be nearly impossible to identify as the delivery mechanism without knowing to look for them
