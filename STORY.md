## 📖 PROLOGUE: THE FUNDING-ROUND PARTY

### The Victim: Nikhil

Nikhil was many things to many people at TripleSpeed — and almost none of them good. As Head of Marketing, he was the smiling face of the company at every all-hands. Behind closed doors he was the asshole who took credit for other people's decks, scapegoated juniors when campaigns flopped, and ran the kind of office politics that left careers in pieces. People put up with him because he made the founders look smart. Numbers went up; bonuses landed; questions weren't asked.

Three months before the party, two things happened that should never have happened in the same week.

First, Nikhil was diagnosed with **Stage 4 pancreatic adenocarcinoma**. Already metastasized. The doctors told him four to six months, palliative only. He told one person at the office: **Alam**, head of HR, his oldest friend in the company.

Second, **SEBI** (Securities and Exchange Board of India) opened a quiet inquiry into TripleSpeed's reported engagement metrics ahead of its Series B due diligence. Someone inside had leaked. The investigators were closing on a single conclusion: that the engagement numbers powering TripleSpeed's flagship "Velocity" dashboard had been doctored at the source. Nikhil was the architect. He would be the first one charged.

Nikhil ran the math. So did Alam.

### The Venue: The Penthouse

The **Penthouse** on the 4th floor of an Indiranagar tower in Bangalore had been home for two years to Sukhans Asrani, Elias Bothell, and Priyanshu — TripleSpeed's two co-founders and their long-time roommate. It was the unofficial second office. Pitch rehearsals happened there. Bad ideas got drunk-killed there. So when Sukhans and Elias decided to throw the **Series B announcement night** at the Penthouse instead of a venue, nobody was surprised.

The plan: invite the entire TripleSpeed office (32 people) on the evening of **May 23, 2026**. Champagne. The big number. A short speech from each founder. Then a thank-you toast from Nikhil to "the team that built it." Then mingling until someone broke something.

### The Guest List: 32 Colleagues

Every guest was a TripleSpeed employee. Engineers, designers, video editors, ops, sales, marketing — the full org. Most of them had at least one private grievance with Nikhil. Stolen credit. Killed projects. Scapegoated launches. Promotion politics that went his way and not theirs. They came because it was a Series B announcement. They didn't come for him.

Three people, however, had reasons that ran deeper than office politics. And one of them was about to commit a murder.

## 🎭 ACT ONE: THE GATHERING (5:30 PM – 7:45 PM)

### The Setup

Alam arrived at the Penthouse at **5:30 PM**, ostensibly to "help HR-prep the swag bags" — branded TripleSpeed hoodies, a printed deck, a bottle of single malt for each senior employee. The roommates were out picking up the catering. For roughly **twenty-five minutes** Alam was alone in the Penthouse.

He used those minutes for one thing.

Nikhil's vape lived in the spare bedroom. He'd been crashing at the Penthouse off and on for months — late-night strategy sessions with Elias, post-investor-call decompressions — and his personal vape pen had become a fixture on the spare-room nightstand. A standard pod-style device, refillable cartridge, the kind every marketing exec in Bangalore carries.

Alam swapped the cartridge for an identical one, pre-loaded with **sodium azide dissolved in the vape juice**. He pocketed the clean cartridge, wiped the device down, set it back exactly where he'd found it. Then he went to make tea.

The roommates returned at 5:55 PM. The catering arrived at 6:30. By 7:00, guests were trickling in.

### The Bar, The Vape Station, The Kitchen

The Penthouse was set up like every Bangalore tech-bro housewarming the office had ever attended:

- **Bar counter** in the living room — champagne for the announcement, whiskey afterward
- **A communal vape station** on the coffee table — guests dropped their personal devices there to free up their hands
- **The kitchen** — catering staff plus a rotating cast of TripleSpeed people stealing samosas
- **The balcony** — smokers, the people avoiding the smokers, and a great view of the Indiranagar skyline

Around 7:15, Nikhil dropped his vape on the coffee-table tray on his way to the bar. It would sit there, in a row of nearly-identical pen-style vapes, for the next forty minutes.

### Guest Arrivals (7:00 PM – 7:45 PM)

The whole office showed up. Engineering arrived early because they always do. Marketing came together. The video editors came late because Tushar was "almost there" for the third time. Ops came pre-coordinated. The designers (Prerna, Vidi) arrived with thoughts about the lighting.

The mood was loud, festive, and tight. Everybody knew the round was big. Nobody knew how big until the founders said so.

## 🥂 ACT TWO: THE ANNOUNCEMENT (8:00 PM – 8:25 PM)

### The Speech

At **8:00 PM sharp**, Sukhans and Elias took the makeshift stage — a slightly-raised section of the living room with a mic on a stand. Behind them, taped to the wall, was the TripleSpeed logo and a giant printed "B" cut out of cardboard.

Sukhans went first. Brief, dry, ego-on-display in his classic "quiet, narcissistic, delusional" register: *"You all know what we're announcing tonight. So I won't milk it. ₹400 crore. Westland Capital is leading. The round closes Friday. None of this happens without every single one of you."*

Elias went second, in his loud "Big American Ginger" energy: *"We made it, you fuckers. Series B. SERIES BEEEE. Drink fast, drink expensive, and somebody please not let me get on the keyboard tonight."*

Champagne corks popped. People hugged people they didn't usually hug.

Then **Nikhil** took the mic.

### Nikhil's Toast

He looked thinner than people remembered. He'd been "on a cleanse" for months — that's what he'd told the office. His hair looked a little dustier than usual; his suit hung a little looser. But he had his energy on.

"I know I'm a difficult person to work with," he said. The room half-laughed. "I push hard. I take credit. I scapegoat. Some of you have wanted to kill me on at least one Tuesday." Bigger laugh.

"But I want to say something I've never said. I don't deserve this team. I don't deserve the year you all just had. Most importantly — I don't deserve the credit Sukhans and Elias are about to give me, on stage, in about thirty seconds, when they hand me this cheque." He held up a prop oversized cheque. "So before they do — to the team. To every fake-ass dashboard you helped me make real. To every late-night Slack message I owe you an apology for. Cheers."

He hit his vape. Once. To laughs.

He hit it again, longer this time, and held up his champagne glass.

The room toasted. Everyone drank.

### Mingling (8:18 PM – 8:40 PM)

After the toast, the announcement officially ended and the party began. Nikhil circulated. He took hits between conversations — three, four, maybe five total. The sodium azide didn't act instantly; it built. Cellular respiration was failing in slow motion while he laughed at Mihir Yadav's "I am Batman" routine for the hundredth time.

At **8:25 PM** there was a brief power flicker — the building's maintenance team had warned about it earlier in the week. It knocked out the hallway CCTV for about three minutes. Nobody at the party noticed.

By **8:40 PM**, Nikhil had drifted to the balcony. He sat down on the stone bench. His face was waxy. His eyes wouldn't focus. Vipin saw him first and said something like *"bro you good?"*

Nikhil was not good.

## 💀 ACT THREE: THE COLLAPSE (8:45 PM)

At **8:45 PM**, Nikhil slumped forward off the balcony bench and hit the floor.

The room broke. Someone screamed. Alam — already strategically positioned near the balcony door — was the first one beside him, calling his name, loosening his collar, taking charge of the chaos. Within two minutes the whole office had clustered into a horrible silent ring around the balcony. Mohit was hyperventilating. Neha was on the phone with the ambulance. Yash Shindey, in pure professional autopilot, took photographs of the scene before he could stop himself.

**9:02 PM:** the paramedics pronounced Nikhil dead at the scene. Cyanosis around the lips. Pupils fixed and dilated. Cardiac arrest secondary to suspected acute toxic exposure.

**9:30 PM:** Bangalore Police arrived. Inspector Reema Mathur of the Indiranagar division took charge. The Penthouse was sealed. Nobody left.

"All thirty-two of you," she said, looking at the assembled office, "are going to give me a statement before I let any one of you go home."

## 🔬 THE TRUTH: WHAT REALLY HAPPENED

### The Diagnosis

Three months before the party, Nikhil got the news. Stage 4 pancreatic. Already in the liver and lymph nodes. Four to six months. Palliative only. He didn't tell his family. He didn't tell the founders. He told **Alam**, because Alam had been his friend since their first job together, and because Alam was the only person he trusted to hold a secret.

### The Fraud

What Alam already knew — what only Alam knew, because Alam had been helping him hide it — was that TripleSpeed's flagship "Velocity" dashboard had been quietly cooking its top-line metrics for the better part of two years. Engagement numbers inflated. Retention curves smoothed. The kind of fraud that's invisible if everybody on the inside agrees not to look, and that's nuclear the moment a regulator does look.

In **mid-January 2026**, somebody on the inside leaked. SEBI opened a preliminary inquiry. The summons hadn't landed yet, but the writing was on the wall. Nikhil was the architect; Alam, as Head of HR, had been the one quietly cleaning up the equity grants and "performance bonuses" that made the fraud's incentive structure work. They were both going to be charged. Possibly first-named.

### The Math

Nikhil ran every option:

| Option | Insurance | SEBI case | Reputation | Family | Alam |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Die naturally of cancer | Pays out (no suicide) | Continues posthumously, full discovery on his estate | Destroyed | Inherits a fraud judgment | Goes to jail |
| Suicide | **Voids** suicide clause (₹2 Cr personal + ₹5 Cr key-person policy) | Continues, family + estate fully exposed | Destroyed | Inherits debt + judgment | Goes to jail |
| **Staged murder** | **Pays out fully** | **Dies with the prime suspect** | Repaired (he's a victim) | Provided for | **Walks** |

The third option was the only one that worked for both of them.

### The Plan

The plan came together over six weeks. Alam handled logistics. Nikhil handled appearances.

1. **The poison** — sodium azide. Alam sourced it through a private contact. Multiple people in the office had plausible alternative access (Pranav's freelance investigation contacts, Yash Shindey's insurance/forensics work, Mohit's cyber-curiosities) — built-in red herrings.

2. **The vape** — Nikhil's personal vape pen, conveniently a device he'd been seen using publicly for months. Alam pre-loaded an identical replacement cartridge. Swap window: the 25 minutes Alam had alone in the Penthouse while the roommates got catering. Forensics would later detect azide in the cartridge — but by then there'd be a queue of suspects who'd had access to the coffee-table vape station.

3. **The night** — Series B announcement. Nikhil had to be alive long enough to give his thank-you toast on stage; the dose was calibrated to begin acting after he'd taken three or four hits. Time to death from first hit: roughly 25 minutes. Time to last hit before collapse: roughly 5 minutes. By the time he hit the floor, eight or nine other people would have plausible motive, opportunity, and access.

4. **The accomplice's role** — Alam would be the calmest person at the scene, the one who took charge, the one who "gave a statement and went home." HR-shaped composure. Nobody would think twice.

5. **The payout** — the personal life-insurance policy paid Alam's offshore account directly (Alam had quietly named himself executor and partial beneficiary on a renewal Nikhil signed last September; Nikhil never read past the signature page). The TripleSpeed key-person policy paid the company. Both would be processed through HR, which Alam ran.

6. **The fraud case** — with Nikhil dead as a homicide victim and not a fugitive, SEBI's case against TripleSpeed lost its prime suspect. The discovery process narrowed dramatically. Alam's name would never come up.

### Why It Works (And Why It's Not Obvious)

- The vape cartridge swap happened **hours before the party**, before any of the suspects even arrived
- The communal vape station gave **eight or nine people** plausible access
- The 8:25 PM CCTV power flicker was a coincidence — but a *useful* coincidence, because it gave investigators a "blind spot" to chase that has nothing to do with the actual crime
- Multiple suspects have **independent fraud motives**: Vadini's SEO numbers were laundered; Ishika's email account was being used; Mohit built the dashboards; Elias's name is on the SEBI filings; Sukhans loses everything if the round dies; Neha wrote the press copy
- Multiple suspects have **independent personal motives**: Yash Shindey was being blackmailed by Nikhil; Pranav was investigating him; Priyanshu had been threatened by him at home
- Even Alam has a clean public motive: "He bullied my juniors."

The only person who could connect Alam to the swap is Nikhil. Nikhil is dead.
