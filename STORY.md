## 1. The world

The case takes place at **Greenr, Assagao, Goa**, on **19 September 2026**, during a private sunset signing dinner. The room is not an office. It is a temporary coalition of founders, collaborators, advisers, designers, engineers, coaches, health people, media people and venue operators trying to close a Greenr launch vehicle before dinner goes cold.

Everyone in the room has put in something real: money, work, reputation, leverage, access, or a promise they now need the signing to justify.

Greenr's useful physical geography:

- **Courtyard** - the signing table, the welcome mic, the sound desk, the main bar line.
- **Upstairs library** - where Rehan goes to review paper in quiet.
- **Upstairs tea shelf** - a library alcove holding Rehan's bottle and a self-serve communal tonic decanter, filled before the welcome line.
- **Herb walk / parking curve** - decorative yellow-oleander hedge and the service route past it.
- **AV booth** - where the launch reel and the camera capture are controlled.
- **Pantry / service stair** - the invisible route staff can use when guests are staring the wrong way.

At **5:25 PM**, rain starts hard enough that the side gate is chained and the valet lane narrows to one controlled exit. Nobody leaves after that. The upstairs decanter is filled at 5:50 PM, before the crash; no server brings Rehan a private drink. By the time police arrive, the weather is part of the lock.

---

## 2. The victim

**Rehan Vora**, 38 - an independent diligence partner hired quietly by the lead backers to bless the numbers before closing.

Publicly, he is introduced as the man "cleaning the packet up before sunset." Privately, he is the last person between sloppy startup myth and actual signatures.

Three facts everybody on site knew about him:

1. He carried one **matte-black steel bottle** everywhere.
2. Before a formal moment, he always rinsed it and refilled it himself from the upstairs tea shelf.
3. He asked questions like a man who already knew where the answer hurt.

He was due to pause the signing if the numbers were not defensible. He died before he got to do it.

## 3. What Rehan found

Rehan's work split the room into **three criminal lanes** and **seven ugly-but-civil problems**.

### The three criminal lanes

1. **Paper - Jack**
   Hidden carry paper, mirrored through sham advisory retainers and side letters that should never have existed.

2. **Picture - Arun**
   Ghost production retainers, recycled reels billed as new sponsor deliverables, and inflated output built on reused footage.

3. **Pour - Manasi**
   Duplicate beverage invoicing, returnable stock treated as consumed, and shell-distributor buyback credits that vanished before they hit the event ledger.

### The seven red-herring problems

- **Anushka** - founder-float reimbursements and dilution panic
- **Anmoll** - undocumented bridge use of launch float
- **Nilisha** - inflated RSVP and sponsor-commit numbers
- **Amrusha** - exclusivity promised before contract clearance
- **Saima** - engineering shortcut in the guest stack
- **Umair** - reimbursement-dashboard rounding mask that reads badly
- **Sampada** - certification and waiver issues tied to the wellness programming

That split is the reason the case works. Ten people have reasons to fear the signing. Only three solve that fear with murder.

## 4. The conspiracy

The conspiracy forms because Rehan's diligence is not a rumour. It is a packet.

Jack sees the first close-review queries the previous afternoon. They are enough to show him that the room will hear his lane, Arun's lane and Manasi's lane inside the same correction. He does not need to persuade them of danger. The paper has already done that. Rehan's final packet at 12:14 PM the next day turns that contingency into an order to act.

The three jobs break cleanly:

| Killer | Public role | Murder job | What they contribute |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jack** | Deal counsel | **The plan** | Reads the corrected packet first, recruits the other two, builds the Nilisha frame, stages the paper panic |
| **Arun** | Launch film director | **The blind spot** | Schedules the AV sync at 4:46 PM, blinds the live feeds, deletes the buffered corridor clip, loops the reel, drags the guest network down |
| **Manasi** | Beverage partner | **The dose** | Uses the pantry service key, carries the concentrate inside an ordinary dropper and paints it inside Rehan's bottle at 6:02 PM |

They do not need to invent chaos. The room already contains it.

## 5. Why murder, and why this method

### Why murder

Because Rehan is no longer in a negotiable stage. He is not asking for clarifications. He is preparing to pause the signing and read three criminal lines into a room full of backers and collaborators.

Delay no longer solves the problem. Once the correction is spoken aloud, the raise freezes and the paper starts living outside the room.

### Why the bottle

Because the communal drink is too public and too dangerous. The black bottle is perfect:

- it belongs only to Rehan,
- he refills it himself,
- it is left unattended briefly but predictably,
- and nobody else in the room has any reason to drink from it.

### Why yellow oleander

Because Greenr already contains the poison. The decorative hedge by the parking curve provides the source, the herb-walk tools provide the cutting instrument, and a washed bitters dropper provides a container that never looks like evidence until it is too late. Manasi harvested and reduced it the previous evening, when all three already knew Rehan's diligence could stop the close.

The room supplies the poison, the route and the camouflage.

## 6. The day - canonical timeline

### Before the room fills

- **1:05 PM, 18 September** - Jack creates the three-person group chat after seeing the first close-review queries.
- **Night before** - Jack builds a draft Nilisha frame; Manasi clips yellow oleander from the parking hedge and reduces it into a concentrate in a brown dropper bottle.
- **12:14 PM** - Rehan sends Jack the corrected diligence packet with a clear warning: the signing may have to pause.
- **5:22 PM** - Jack refreshes the forged Nilisha packet with the day's welcome-board language and prints its late folder pages.
- **4:46 PM** - Arun schedules the AV sync that will later knock out the cameras and drag the guest network down with it.

### In public view

- **5:15 PM** - check-in opens; the courtyard fills.
- **5:47 PM** - Rehan rinses his bottle and leaves it empty on the upstairs tea shelf.
- **5:58 PM** - the AV sync hits. The live camera feeds and monitors go blind, while the mezzanine system continues buffering locally. Guest Wi-Fi and the card reader fail. Saima and Umair sprint toward the guest stack. The room breaks in useful directions.

### The murder window

- **6:02 PM** - while the live feeds are down, Manasi uses the pantry service stair and paints the inside of Rehan's empty bottle. Jack holds Anushka and Anmoll at the long table over a false signature panic. Arun loops the reel and later kills the buffered corridor clip.
- **6:08 PM** - Rehan returns upstairs, tops up the bottle from the self-serve communal tonic decanter and carries the closing file into the library.
- **6:18 PM** - Nathan finds him on the library floor; Sharon reaches him seconds later.

### The seal

- **6:31 PM** - likely poisoning is spoken out loud.
- **6:40 PM** - police seal Greenr. Rain, the chain and the register keep every player on site.

## 8. Misdirection

This case has four major false closures:

1. **The founder closure** - Anushka and Anmoll both have real reasons to dread the signing.
2. **The vanity closure** - Nilisha really did inflate numbers, so the forged packet lands cleanly.
3. **The systems closure** - the guest stack visibly fails under Saima and Umair's hands.
4. **The service closure** - Amrusha and Sampada both have access-shaped guilt without murder-shaped proof.

Each one is true enough to hold the room. None of them closes the bottle, the sync and the frame at the same time.

---

## 9. How the room solves it

### Round 3 does the first hard turn

Players learn:

- the poison is in Rehan's bottle, not the communal tonic,
- the bottle was dosed while empty after 5:47 and before 6:08,
- the AV crash was scheduled at 4:46,
- the guest-stack failure was collateral, not cause,
- the Nilisha packet was forged before the murder,
- and the yellow hedge at Greenr is part of the mechanism.

### Round 4 separates fear from guilt

Rehan's memo makes the key distinction explicit: **three criminal lanes, seven embarrassing ones**.

That frees the room from choosing between "everybody looks guilty" and "nobody can be solved."

### Round 5 closes the team

The recovered chat proves coordination and the beneficiary map proves the money convergence. Once those land, the question is not whether there was a team. It is whether the room can attach the right three names to paper, picture and pour.

## 10. Canon rulings

1. **The communal tonic is innocent.** Pujah mixed it, the batch tested clean and the bar logs agree.
2. **The AV crash is staged, not accidental.** It is a real interruption with a false cause.
3. **Nilisha is framed, not spotless.** Her inflated numbers are real. The sponsor packet is not.
4. **Anushka and Anmoll are guilty of panic, not murder.** Their paperwork sins are fuel, not solution.
5. **Sampada's tears are genuine.** Shivangi can partly clear her, but only by exposing why she was crying.
6. **No outsider did this.** The weather, gate chain and register lock the answer inside Greenr.
7. **Jack never needed to touch the bottle.** The plan and the frame are actions, not absences.
8. **Arun's alibi is the point.** His decisive acts are scheduled before the poison goes in; the shifted screen wall blocked the courtyard camera's service-stair sightline, while a separate mezzanine recorder kept a local buffer. That is why deleting the corridor clip still mattered.
9. **Manasi's murder weapon is ordinary until the room learns to read it.** The dropper looks like bar gear right up until it becomes evidence. The missing service-stair key is the route, not a second mystery.
10. **The rapid collapse is not a second dose.** The field screen records a highly concentrated cardiac-glycoside exposure; Rehan first drank at 6:08 and was found ten minutes later.

---

## 11. The answer in one breath

**Jack** planned it, **Arun** created the blind spot, and **Manasi** dosed Rehan's black bottle with yellow-oleander concentrate while the room solved a crash they themselves had staged.
