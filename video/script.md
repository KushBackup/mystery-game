# Astral Project — Murder Mystery · Voiceover script

**Runtime** 130s · 3900 frames @ 30fps · **Length** ~325 words (~150 wpm)

The film is built to work **silent** — assume it autoplays muted on a laptop and
the kinetic type carries the whole argument. This script is additive, not
load-bearing. Nothing here says anything the frame does not already say.

## Recording notes

- Dry, unhurried, close-mic. Detective-procedural, not ad-voice. No upsell lift
  at the end of sentences.
- Leave the gaps. Several scenes are designed to land in silence — scene 2's
  five static cliques and scene 10's synchronised turn especially. Do not talk
  over the reveal.
- Word counts below are budgets, not targets. If a line runs long, cut words
  rather than speeding up.

## Fact discipline

Every number spoken here appears in `pitch-deck/index.html` and is true: 32
players, 7 host-gated rounds, a 2–3 hour runtime, 1 host, 0 professional actors,
0 app-store installs, 34 clue codes across five categories, 6 case files, 10
characters under suspicion, 1 murderer.

**There is no revenue, pricing, ticket, attendance, customer, testimonial,
rating, funding or headcount claim anywhere in this script, and none may be
added.** One genre has shipped; five are roadmap, and the script says so.

## To wire it up

Record to `public/vo.mp3`, then flip `hasVO` to `true` in the composition's
`defaultProps` (`src/Root.tsx`) or pass `--props='{"hasVO":true}'` at render
time. With `hasVO: false` the film renders identically, minus the audio track.

## 01 · Cold open — 0:00–0:08 (frames 0–240) · ~20 words

Thirty-two strangers. One story. Every player holds a piece nobody else has —
and the only way out is to talk to each other.

## 02 · The problem — 0:08–0:20 (frames 240–600) · ~30 words

Putting people in a room does not make them meet. Parties, offsites, mixers: they
all fail the same way. People arrive in a group, and they leave in the same group.

*(Hold through the clusters forming. Say nothing after "same group" — the
stillness is the point.)*

## 03 · The mechanism — 0:20–0:29 (frames 600–870) · ~22 words

So we distribute the story. Unique information, to every single player. Talking to
a stranger stops being social bravery and becomes the optimal move.

## 04 · What it is — 0:29–0:39 (frames 870–1170) · ~25 words

Thirty-two guests. Seven rounds. Three hours. One host. No actors, no app-store
download, no set to build. Everyone logs in as a character in a single murder case.

## 05 · You become someone — 0:39–0:49 (frames 1170–1470) · ~25 words

A QR code at the door. A private access code. Then you are someone else — with a
bio, a timeline, and one secret you will lie to protect.

## 06 · The loop — 0:49–1:01 (frames 1470–1830) · ~30 words

This is the loop the whole evening runs on. Get a code from a person. Type it in.
Watch the case change. The printed card is the social object; the app is what
makes it mean something.

## 07 · Codes are social objects — 1:01–1:09 (frames 1830–2070) · ~20 words

And a code has to be obtained from someone. The app cannot hand it over. That
single rule is what forces the room to talk.

## 08 · Seven rounds — 1:09–1:20 (frames 2070–2400) · ~27 words

Seven rounds, one release valve at a time. The incident. Accusations. Motives.
Evidence. Revelations. Bombshells. A code entered too early is simply rejected, so
nobody outruns the story.

## 09 · The verdict — 1:20–1:29 (frames 2400–2670) · ~22 words

Then voting opens, and the count moves in the open. Public commitment, publicly
visible. Ten characters are under suspicion. One of them did it.

## 10 · The reveal — 1:29–1:36 (frames 2670–2880) · ~17 words

One host. One button. Thirty-two screens. Every screen in the venue turns at the
same instant.

*(Then silence. Let the flood and the cut to black play dry.)*

## 11 · Host control — 1:36–1:44 (frames 2880–3120) · ~20 words

Because the app is the game master, the host is only the MC. Rounds, voting, file
releases, the ending — all from one pocket.

## 12 · Why it works — 1:44–1:54 (frames 3120–3420) · ~25 words

Six mechanisms do the social work. Information asymmetry. Forced reciprocity.
Identity cover. Paced revelation. Public commitment. And a shared story the room
keeps arguing about for weeks.

## 13 · The platform — 1:54–2:04 (frames 3420–3720) · ~25 words

Murder Mystery is the first world, not the company. One story has shipped; five
more are mapped. Because content is data, not code, a new world is a writing job.

## 14 · Close — 2:04–2:10 (frames 3720–3900) · ~15 words

Trust no one. Except, briefly, the thirty-one people you just spent three hours
interrogating.
